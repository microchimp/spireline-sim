## Symptom

Variant: add: the emulator deck holds one more card than the referee deck and no card is missing (the first pick of the screen put a card into the emulator deck at once; the game adds it when the screen closes).

Group weight 105.76, 19 divergence(s), 44 decisions affected, first at floor 3, 10 outcome-changing confirmed; all records are roots (no earlier divergence in their run). In every record the diff is the single field `.deck` and equals the effect of the previous pick of the same screen, which is the mark of a mid-screen pick (the driver asks one decision per card, k decisions for a k-card screen). The record then suspends the lockstep (`Lockstep._lose`), so every decision up to the next room entry or fight start is scored not ranked: that is where the weight comes from, not from a wrong rule.

## Expected behaviour (game)
The driver (`referee/driver/RunPlay.cs`, class `RunSelector`, method `GetSelectedCards`) is the card selector the game calls for every card-choice screen outside a fight (the game's `CardSelectCmd` family: `FromDeckForRemoval`, `FromDeckForUpgrade`, `FromDeckForTransformation`, the simple-grid and choose-a-card screens). For a screen that needs k cards (k = the screen's minimum: 2 or more for a two-card removal, upgrade or transform, or a reward grid of several picks) `Env.DecideMany` (`referee/driver/Env.cs`) asks k separate decisions of kind `select_cards`, each with context `{pick: i, of: k}` and the cards still left as options. The game gets the picks back as one list only after the last pick, and its caller then applies the effect to all of them (remove from deck, upgrade, transform, add to deck; the follow-up such as gold or max HP last). So at the decisions pick 2 to k the game deck, as the driver reports it in the observation, is still the deck from before the screen. The same fact for fight card screens is already written down in `referee/lockstep.py`, function `mid_screen_pick` (the referee moves the picked cards only when the screen closes, the emulator after each pick).

## Emulator mechanism and its other uses
The emulator is right and must not change. `StepTransformSelect` (`src/Sts2Emulator/Core/Run/RunEngine.cs`, about line 5558) applies each pick at once through `RunNonCombatEffects.ApplyDeckSelection` (it decrements `PendingSelectionCount`, about line 1982) and stays on the screen while picks are owed and a selectable card is left; `StepOfferSelect` (same file, about line 5203) adds the picked offered card to the deck at once and counts `PendingOfferPicks` down. After pick i the emulator deck therefore holds i effects, the game's none. Multi-pick screens in the emulator include the two-card transform and removal events, the cook-at-rest removal, a merchant event's two upgrades, and offer grids with more than one pick (`RunRewardGenerator.CardFactory.cs`, about line 241, `PendingOfferPicks = picks`).

The defect is the comparer. `mid_screen_pick` (`referee/lockstep.py`, line 182) is true only for `select_cards_combat`. Its three users: `Lockstep._equal` (about line 346, skips the structural diff), `EmuLock._screen_state` (about line 627, compares the screen's candidates) and `referee_only_policy` (about line 948, answers "done"). For kind `select_cards` it is false, so `_equal` runs `structural_diff` on a deck that is one effect ahead on the emulator side, `_check` logs a `select_cards: .deck` record and `_lose` suspends the lockstep. `_resume` (a lost lockstep looking for a place to resume) also goes through `_equal`, so it has the same blind spot. Keep right: the combat uses of `mid_screen_pick` must not change (do not widen that function; add a sibling); pick 1 of a screen (no effect applied yet) and a message without `ctx` must still be compared in full; the other fields (hp, max hp, gold, potions, relics, floor) must still be compared at pick 2 and later, because a real difference there is not an artefact. One caveat from the emulator source: a cost the game charges after the selector (the gold of a shop removal) the emulator may charge when the screen opens; if the landing run shows a gold diff at pick 2 or later, widen the skipped fields to gold and say so.

## Fix sketch
1. `referee/lockstep.py`: add `deck_pending_pick(kind, msg)` next to `mid_screen_pick`: true when kind is `select_cards` and `msg["ctx"]` is a dict with an integer `pick` greater than 1. Do not touch `mid_screen_pick`.
2. In `Lockstep._equal`, after `ref_view` and `emu.view()`, when `deck_pending_pick(kind, msg)` holds, drop every diff entry whose path starts with `.deck` and keep all other entries, including a non-empty `reason` from `emu.sync`.
3. Optional stricter form: instead of dropping the deck, accept it only when the two decks differ by at most 2*(pick-1) cards as multisets, so a gross deck error at pick 2 still shows; use it if cheap.
4. Update the `mid_screen_pick` docstring to name the non-combat sibling and why the emulator is ahead by design. No change to `EmuLock.sync` (its screen-type check, `card_select`, stays and still reports an emulator that closed its screen early).
5. No C# change, no emulator change.

## Confidence
High that the mechanism is the one named for the transform, remove and upgrade groups (the diff equals exactly the effect of the previous pick, the driver asks one decision per pick and returns the picks together, the emulator applies each pick at once, and the combat sibling exists for the same reason). Medium for the add groups: they fit the same pattern (an offered card put into the emulator deck by the first pick of a multi-pick grid) but the records do not show which grid or relic opened the screen. What would raise it: a one-line probe in the landing session that prints `ctx` (`pick`, `of`), the action labels and the kind of the previous decision at the 19 flagged decisions; pick 2 or higher in all 19 settles it. If some record shows pick 1, the emulator deck was ahead for another reason (an auto-resolved or duplicated screen) and that record needs its own trace.
