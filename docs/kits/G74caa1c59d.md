# Kit G74caa1c59d (rank 21, weight 46.2, family F15 by the ranking doc; the mechanism is a Thieving Hopper steal). Shared body for G8318fb1cf5, G88f7b7316f, Gb1aa8d4875, G5b5244da1e

## Symptom
Five groups, one record each (run, decision): 9BKD2MYRNJ#168 floor 19 (the emulator deck has BULLY, the referee deck does not), LL75B9MPHB#155 floor 19 (SPITE), TN58QWDEL2#157 floor 19 (PILLAGE), 5MG5H69EKK#167 floor 20 (INFLAME), 42DBNHQDDW#147 floor 19 (WHIRLWIND). In every record the screen is a combat, the encounter is THIEVING_HOPPER, the turn is 2 (the first decision after the Hopper's first enemy phase), and the whole structural diff is one `.deck` entry: the emulator's run deck holds one card more than the referee's (24 entries against 23 in the first record, same pattern in the rest). The difference stays for the rest of the fight (the not-ranked span is 9 to 18 decisions, to the fight's end) and is gone after it. Total weight of the five is 175.4, the whole R16 bucket of the ranking doc.

The ranking doc's reading (R16: "a card taken from a reward on one side only", i.e. the Act 1 boss reward pick) is not what the records show. Evidence: all five are THIEVING_HOPPER turn 2; the differing card is a different one each time; TN58QWDEL2 has no earlier divergence at all, and the other four have earlier select_cards or combat divergences at other floors that do not touch the deck at the Hopper. A boss-reward card taken on one side only would be missing at every later screen, not for one fight. These are root records, not consequences of the earlier divergences.

## Expected behaviour (game)
`ThievingHopper` opens with its THIEVERY move, which steals one card and hangs it on a `SwipePower` on the Hopper. `SwipePower.Steal(card)` records the card and, when the card has a deck version, calls `CardPileCmd.RemoveFromDeck(card.DeckVersion, showPreview: false)` at once. So the player's master deck is one card smaller from that moment to the end of the fight, and every deck read in that time sees it gone. `SwipePower.BeforeDeath` (only when the Hopper dies, not when it escapes) registers the deck version with the run and hangs a `SpecialCardReward` of it on the combat room: the reward row the emulator already models. A stolen card with no deck version (a card made in the fight) removes nothing and returns nothing.

## Emulator mechanism and its other uses
- `src/Sts2Emulator/Core/EnemyAI.cs` (the steal, near line 4730): picks the card from the draw or discard pile, stores it on `EnemyState.StolenCard`, applies `BuffId.Swipe`, adds it to `CombatState.StolenCards`. Correct; no change.
- `src/Sts2Emulator/Core/Run/RunEngine.cs`, `TakeStolenCardsFromTheDeck` (called first in `FinishCombat`): removes the stolen cards from `State.Deck` only when the combat ends (match the deck version by value, then by id+enchantment+tinker, then by id) and moves the ones the Hopper died holding into `StolenBackCards`. The comment on `CombatState.StolenCards` says the run "takes its deck version out as the combat ends (a combat cannot reach the run's deck)". That is the cause: during the fight `State.Deck` still holds the card, so any reader of the deck in a fight sees one card too many.
- The readers that matter in a fight: (1) `tools/emudump/RunSnap.cs` `Export` writes `deck` from `s.Deck` (the lockstep's emulator side; the same method already reads the player's hp from the fight state during a fight, for the same reason, so the precedent is there); (2) `RunEngine.WriteDeckObservation` (the agent-facing deck slots), the deck count at `obs[CombatObsSize + 3]`, and `info[3]`.
- Other users of the mechanism that the fix must keep right: `FinishCombat` (the card leaves the deck exactly once, never twice); `DebugResolveCombat(stolenCards:, stolenBackCards:)` (the test and replay hook that injects a theft); `RunRewardGenerator` (adds `StolenBackCards` as `RewardSpecialCards` rows) and claiming a row (a deck add); `CombatSnapshot` keys `stolen_cards` / `stolen_back_cards` and `StateCloning` (they already carry the lists, so a view computed on demand needs no new state and no snapshot change); `src/Sts2Emulator.Tests/StolenLootRewardTests.cs` and `Combats/HiveWeakEncounterTests.cs` (`KillingTheHopperReturnsTheStolenCard` and the escape test) which pin the end-of-combat behaviour. Do not move the removal into `State.Deck` mid-fight: the combat was started from `State.Deck`, and the rebuild lane (Core2) is off limits.

## Fix sketch
1. `RunEngine`: add `public IReadOnlyList<CardInstance> DeckInFight()` returning `State.Deck` unless `State.Phase == RunPhase.Combat`, `State.ActiveCombat` is set and its `StolenCards` is not empty; then a copy of `State.Deck` with one slot removed per stolen card. Pull the three-tier match out of `TakeStolenCardsFromTheDeck` into one helper that takes the deck slots already used (so five identical Strikes are matched one to one) and call it from both places. A stolen card with no match removes nothing from the view (the game removes a deck version only).
2. Use the view in `WriteDeckObservation`, in `obs[offset + 3]` and in `info[3]`.
3. `tools/emudump/RunSnap.cs` `Export`: write `deck` from `e.DeckInFight()`, with a short comment that it is the same reason as the hp line above it.
4. `tools/fidelity/run_snapshot.py`: the `NOT_COMPARED` entry about a Hopper's stolen card says the emulator returns the card to the deck as the fight ends; reword it (comment only) to say the deck loses the card at the steal on both sides.
Leave `State.Deck`, `StolenCards`, the snapshot keys and `FinishCombat` as they are.
